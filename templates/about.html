About EarlyWarn

Predicting risk before the first class

EarlyWarn is an AI-powered early-warning system that uses only enrollment-time data to flag students at higher risk of dropping out — giving counselors a head start on intervention before academic struggles even begin.

🎯 What is EarlyWarn?

early-warning system

Most dropout prediction tools rely on semester grades and attendance — data that only becomes available after a student is already falling behind. EarlyWarn is different: it uses information known at the moment of enrollment — application details, academic background, family socioeconomic context, financial status, and macroeconomic indicators — to produce a calibrated dropout-risk probability.

The system is trained on the real UCI "Predict Students' Dropout and Academic Success" dataset (Realinho et al., 2021) covering 4,424 students from a Portuguese higher-education institution.

⚡

Pre-enrollment flagging

Identifies at-risk students before they attend a single class, enabling proactive outreach and support allocation.

🔍

Explainable predictions

Every prediction comes with a SHAP-based explanation showing which factors drove the risk score — no black-box guessing.

📊

Calibrated probabilities

Outputs aren't just labels — they're probability-calibrated scores so counselors can prioritize students by actual risk level.

🛡️

Screening aid, not verdict

Designed as a decision-support tool that complements — never replaces — professional counselor judgment and student dialogue.

📚 Dataset

training data source

UCI "Predict Students' Dropout and Academic Success" — a real-world dataset of 4,424 students from a Portuguese polytechnic institute, published by Realinho et al. (2021). The dataset includes 36 features covering demographics, academic history, socioeconomic background, and macroeconomic context at the time of enrollment.

For the EarlyWarn system, the original three-class target (Dropout / Enrolled / Graduate) is collapsed into a binary task: Dropout vs. Not at risk (Enrolled + Graduate), which aligns directly with the intervention question counselors need answered.
